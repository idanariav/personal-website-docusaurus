The Stress Solution (Book)

Note-Making​

Clarify​

🔈 Summary of main ideas

  1. Purpose:
    1. Have a positive frame on line
    2. be grateful
    3. Do something you love
  2. Mind

  3. Relationships
    1. maintain relationships

    3. Schedule time for intimacy
  4. Body
    1. eat healthy
    2. sync with your circadian rhythm
    3. Do some exercise

Relate​

⛓ by following this method, what will happen? What is the goal of this book? less stressful, happy life

Act​

📋What should I do to achieve the goals set out by this book?

  1. Daily affirmation - Repeat one positive statement every day, even when you don't feel like it (e.g., "I handle what comes one step at a time" at the bathroom mirror).
  2. Reframe a stressor - When a "why is this always happening to me" thought shows up, restate the situation as work that benefits others, or view it from outside (e.g., a delayed project becomes "what can I do for the team now?").
  3. Gratitude practice - Daily, note something you're grateful for. Weekly, do a loving-kindness meditation, wishing a good future for someone, ideally someone you dislike (e.g., 5 minutes on Sunday).
  4. Time-block your week - Schedule what matters first, plus a hobby, movement, and solitude or reflection, and open each day with a morning routine (e.g., a Sunday-evening calendar pass with a 30-minute morning "zone-in").
  5. Apply L.I.V.E. - Weekly, do something you love, act with intention, move toward a long-term vision, and engage with others (e.g., greet the cashier, savor the smell of your tea).
  6. Daily consensual touch - Add hugs, high-fives or a gentle pet to your day (e.g., a hug when you get home).
  7. Schedule intimacy - Weekly date night, plus a daily greeting with a smile, eye contact and a touch, and phone-free deep conversations (e.g., phones in a drawer during Friday dinner).
  8. Nurture friendships - Join a club or become a regular somewhere, and meet your closest friends face to face regularly (e.g., a monthly dinner with a close friend).
  9. Eat for your gut - Daily, eat a diverse set of vegetables and cut processed food, sugar and alcohol. Eat most of your food early and stop at least 3 hours before bed (e.g., last meal by 7pm if bed is at 10pm).
  10. Move, sleep with the light, and detox - Exercise regularly without overdoing it, sleep when it's dark, and cut blue light, caffeine and alcohol late in the day. Detox from social media, spend time outdoors, and practice slow belly breathing such as box breathing (e.g., a daily walk and 5 minutes of box breathing after work).

Critique​

🧩 relevant research, metaphors or examples that helps to convey the argument

  1. Mechanism / analogy – The body is "a web of information" and reacts to signals, so each "micro dose of stress" (a fight, the news, being in a hurry) signals "be ready for a threat" and prolongs the stress state. Supports: chronic stress is built from small everyday triggers.
  2. Physiological account – Under stress, adrenaline and cortisol prime muscles, brain, senses and immune system, while digestion shuts down. Fine for short bursts, but long-term it leads to heart attacks, digestion issues and inflammation. Supports: stress is adaptive short-term and harmful when prolonged.
  3. Neuroplasticity argument – The brain learns from experience, so surrounding yourself with threats makes you see everything as stress and lowers your threshold. Supports: small stressors compound over time.
  4. Everyday example – "Facebook brain": FOMO from everyone's apparent happiness, constant criticism of what you post, endless content, and pressure to be always available. Supports: a technology detox.
  5. Counter-intuitive claim – The difference between night owls and morning larks is very small, and we all need to sleep when it's dark. Supports: reset your circadian rhythm.
  6. Causal example – Unhealthy eating or alcohol as a coping strategy for a lack of meaning, so no amount of education helps until the source is treated. Supports: purpose is the root of many health issues.

❌ the logical jumps, holes or simply cases where it is wrong...

  1. Evidence is largely asserted - The notes give no studies or figures for claims like touch strengthening immunity, probiotics helping, or the night owl/lark difference being small. The book seems to rely on authority and plausibility.
  2. "Nothing new" - Affirmations, gratitude, exercise, sleep, nature and breathing are widely known advice. The book's contribution is mostly the packaging (the micro-dose framing, L.I.V.E.).
  3. Repetitive overlap - Mind, body, relationships and purpose are deeply intertwined, so the same advice (move, go outside, connect, be intentional) resurfaces under different headings.
  4. Possible overreach on purpose - Saying a lack of purpose "can be the underlying cause of many health issues" and that education won't help until it's treated at the source is a big causal claim. Bad habits have many other causes, such as genetics, environment and addiction.
  5. Reverse causality on intimacy - The book blames smartphones and economic stress for falling intimacy. Stress can also reduce intimacy, and the notes show no evidence separating the two directions.
  6. Affirmations vs. helplessness - Saying affirmations "even when you don't feel like it" assumes this works. Forced positive statements can backfire for people who don't believe them, and the notes mention no such caveat.
  7. Fasting and probiotics advice is thin - Intermittent fasting and probiotics are recommended in one line, with no evidence or caveats shown in the notes.

🧱 Implementations and limitations of it are...

  1. Time cost for the time-poor - The book admits we lack time, then asks for a morning routine, scheduled intimacy, exercise, friend meetups and nature time. Applying everything is itself a source of pressure.
  2. Stress with structural causes - Habits can't fix stress caused by poverty, unsafe work, caregiving or a bad boss. The book seems to place the solution in the individual.
  3. Needs a partner or social circle - Intimacy, touch and friendship advice assumes a partner or friends. It suits less well people who are isolated, single or grieving, where "schedule a date night" doesn't apply.
  4. Fasting and diet don't suit everyone - Eating early and fasting are poor fits for people with eating disorders, diabetes, pregnancy or shift work.
  5. Shift workers and caregivers - "Sleep when it's dark and wake when it's light" is hard for night-shift workers, new parents and people with irregular schedules.
  6. Exercise can become a stressor - The book itself warns that overdoing it damages the body, but gives no guidance on a safe dose, so people prone to overdoing it may do harm.
  7. Clinical stress and anxiety - Breathing, gratitude and nature suit everyday stress. They are not a substitute for treatment when stress has become an anxiety disorder or depression.

Review​

💭 my opinions on the book, the writers style... This book is a collection of "obvious" facts for less stressful and happier life. Nothing new, nothing unique, very repetitive since mind, body relationships and purpose are all intertwined.

Outline​

Notes​

Introduction​

Stress is now an epidemic, and taking a hold of our lives, affecting our physical and mental health.

Our body has two states: stressed state and thriving state. Being in a state of stress is not necessarily bad. Stress sends adrenaline and Cortisol throughout our body to prepare us in a heightened survival state. If what we need right now is to survive, then stress is good for us. It primes our muscles, our brain, our senses and our immune system to detect and handle danger. Everything that's not survival related such as digestion is shut down. It's good for short term bursts, not long term state. Stress. That long term state is what makes us less healthy and stable, through things like heart attacks, digestion issues, inflammation.

Throughout the day, we experience endless "micro doses of stress" (MDS) that trigger and prolong that stress state. This is because our body is a web of information. Our body reacts to signals it gets, so these MDS send signals of "be ready for facing a threat" every time we see them, for example getting into a fight, watching the news, being in a hurry, etc.

The problem gets worse as we realize that our brain is plastic, it "learns" to improve it's functioning based on experiences Neuroplasticity. If we're getting used to being surrounded by threats, then we'll start to see everything as a stress. Our threshold of what counts as a stress will lower Repeated exposure raises threshold

Purpose​

A lack of purpose can be the underlying cause of many health issues. For example we might use unhealthy eating or alcoholism as a coping strategy for a lack of meaning in life. No amount of education will help unless we treat it at the source.

Treating it requires time, the one thing we lack on modern days since we're occupying ourselves more and more with work.

The 3 Habits of Calm​

  1. Affirmations - A positive statement you repeat often. It's like hacking. your brain into a new positive perspective. Say it daily, even when you don't feel like it. Self affirmation
  2. Reframing - We can't escape the lenses through which we see the world. Since we're bombarded with stress doses, we can default to a helpless mentality framing, like "why is this always happening to me". Instead we should try to reframe our life as a proactive work to benefit others. Also try to look at your situation from an external point of view Reframing
  3. Gratitude - have a practice of gratitude. Even if we don't express it, it changes us. The "loving kindness meditation" where we try to wish someone a good future of flourishing and happiness is great, especially if we wish it for someone we don't really like Gratitude

Schedule Your time​

When we feel like we don't have time for anything, a useful technique is time blocking Time Blocking

It helps us by:

  1. Putting what matters for us first
  2. Finding time for hobby and relaxation, something that brings us joy
  3. Scheduling something that moves our body
  4. Scheduling time for solitude and reflection

It's best to have a morning routine to "zone in" and start the day.

How to Live More​

We are creatures driven by sense of purpose and meaning. It has many names, to matter, ikigai. For simplicity, it offers L.I.V.E:

  1. L - doing what you love, doesn't have to be your job, but something that fills you with joy and excitement
  2. I - intentional. Being mindful of what you do, like focusing on the sensation of a shower, the smell of tea
  3. V - vision, have a long term plan for your life, something you wish to achieve
  4. E - Engagement. Something that connects you with others, small acts of kindness, even just saying hello to the cashier.

Relationships​

Human touch​

Touch is essential for us, the physical manifestation of connection. Our body responses well to touch, strengthening immune system, reduces stress, and releasing oxytocin. consentual touch is an important part of every day life, from a hi-five to a gentle pet, to a hug. It benefits both sides physical touch creates connection

Get Intimate​

Intimacy has gone down recently, due to high exposure to smartphones and increased stress from the economy and life in general.

The lack of intimacy is bad for our relationships as it is bad for our physical and mental health.

Intimacy is not just sex. Sex is often the result, not the cause, of intimacy. To increase the intimacy:

  1. Schedule a date night
  2. Greet each other with a smile, with eye contact, and a touch
  3. Try phone less deep conversations
  4. Spend quality time together - a meal, lying in bed naked, taking a shower together

Nurture Your Friendships​

Loneliness is painful, both physically and mentally. Aside from maintaining your social circle, it's important to expand it. Join a club, go regularly to a cafe at the same time, find your community Third places anchor community connection.

Recognize who are your closest friends, and keep touch with them. Meet them face to face once in a while.

Body​

Eat Yourself Happy​

Much of our mood is affected by what we eat, so dr-stressing our diet is critical Nutrition.

Processed food, sugar, alcohol, smoking and even antibiotics can be very bad for our microbiome and gut health, which can lead to issues such as inflammation, diabetes and depression.

Try to eat a diverse set of vegetables, do intermediate fasting, and take probiotics.

Make Exercise Work for You​

Exercise is great for reducing stress, as long as you're not overdoing it. Overdoing it will both damage your body and become a stresser Exercises.

Exercise is not just running, it's yoga, it's skipping, it's whatever moves your body.

Reset Your Rhythm​

The most important rhythm of our body is the circadian rhythm. Despite what we think, the difference between night owls and morning larks is very small Circadian Rhythm.

We all need to sleep when it's dark and wake when it's light. Pushing ourselves with blue light, caffeine and alcohol is detrimental to our health.

Another important rhythm is food. Its best to stick to intermediate fasting, where we eat most of the good in the morning, and less and less as the day passes, stopping at least three hours before going to bed.

Mind​

Technology Overload​

Recent developments of tech is alarming and harmful. We've grown "Facebook brain". We get fomo from seeing how everyone's is "happy", we get endless criticism for every content we upload.

We get endless content to consume, endless distractions, and pressure to be constantly available.

We're in serious need of Detox. To limit, remove and detach as much as possible from tech.

Bath Yourself in Nature​

Being in nature and looking at nature is the exact opposite of being on your phone, it enriches you, revitalize you Nature walk.

Try to be more outdoors, using pictures of nature.

Also try to declutter your house. A messy home is a messy mind.

Take time to Breathe​

Breathing is a sign and an influencer of our mental state. We're breathing rapidly when we're stressed, but breathing deeply can also be a calming factor. Breath slowly, with your belly, from your nose, full deep breaths. Methods such as the 3-4-5 or box breathing is good for maintaining calmness. A good alternative or complementary to meditation breathing.
