# Forward Thinking

## Notes

### Claim
Whenever we face a [Challenge](/notes/struggle-is-an-inevitable-and-necessary-part-of-any-meaningful-growth-or-achievement.md), we can either dwell on the past, filling ourselves with [Blame is attributing fault instead of owning what's in your control](/notes/blame-is-attributing-fault-instead-of-owning-whats-in-your-control.md) or [We regret inaction more than action taken](/notes/we-regret-inaction-more-than-action-taken.md) for failing to meet our [Expectations shape wellbeing through the gap between desires and reality](/notes/expectations-shape-wellbeing-through-the-gap-between-desires-and-reality.md), or we can focus on the road ahead, what we can do now and how we can act better next time [Optimism is choosing to believe outcomes can improve through action](/notes/optimism-is-choosing-to-believe-outcomes-can-improve-through-action.md).

### Explanation
This is really a specific case of a broader reframe: turning the backward-looking question of why something happened into the forward-looking one of what to do about it [Turn negative emotions into fuel for growth and action](/notes/turn-negative-emotions-into-fuel-for-growth-and-action.md) — the same shift works whether what's pulling us backward is blame, regret, fear, or anger.

### Examples

### Supporters
While the first view hurts our [Self-worth is accurate assessment of your capabilities](/notes/self-worth-is-accurate-assessment-of-your-capabilities.md) and promotes [victimhood](/notes/helplessness-is-perceiving-no-control-over-circumstances-and-outcomes.md), this empowers it, focusing on our [Agency is the ability to connect desires and actions](/notes/agency-is-the-ability-to-connect-desires-and-actions.md).

### Opposers

### Open questions
This note says not to dwell on what went wrong, but [Analyze both results and methods to improve](/notes/analyze-both-results-and-methods-to-improve.md) argues the opposite move — actually reviewing what happened, including a proper post-mortem — has its own value. Where's the line between reflecting to learn and dwelling to punish yourself?
