# Determinism

## Notes

### Claim
Determinism is the idea that theoretically, everything is a result of a chain of events, led by cause and effect.

### Explanation
The easiest field that we believe is deterministic is mechanics. Things move according to the forces that influence them, in a very expectable way. Like when a ball hits another ball, that one didn't have the ability to do anything other than moving in the exact location that can be calculated by physics formulas. [Complex things are best understood by their core parts](/notes/complex-things-are-best-understood-by-their-core-parts.md)

Similarly, other scientific fields such as electricity, chemistry, all are limited by rules that are universal and unbreakable such that it is technically possible to calculate the outcome of each situation.

### Why it matters
If this is true, what are the conclusions for human action? After all, human actions are perhaps just a collection of chemical responses, or electrical signals. Aren't we like the ball that was hit, being forced to act in a certain way? That is the greatest challenge to free will. Maybe that's also why [there's something freeing in accepting what we can't control anyway](/notes/accepting-what-we-cannot-control-is-the-path-to-peace.md).

### Examples

### Supporters
We wish to think that it's wrong, since humans often act in an unpredictable way, but perhaps that's just because [We can't read minds](/notes/understanding-others-requires-attributing-mental-states-like-beliefs-and-intentions-to-them.md), and because life is [complex](/notes/complexity-makes-ideas-harder-to-understand-and-implement.md). We also assume we have [Agency is the ability to connect desires and actions](/notes/agency-is-the-ability-to-connect-desires-and-actions.md) over our actions. That doesn't mean that we are acting in an [indeterministic](/notes/determinism-and-free-will-can-coexist.md) way, but rather we haven't been able to identify all the relevant variables, and find the formula that will produce the exact predictions [Randomness may be an illusion created by incomplete knowledge](/notes/randomness-may-be-an-illusion-created-by-incomplete-knowledge.md).

### Opposers
Stoicism pushes against this — the idea that [The will is always fully within our control](/notes/the-will-is-always-fully-within-our-control.md) treats the will itself as something outside the causal chain, always ours to direct no matter what came before.

### Open questions
Maybe it's all just a façade, like a video game character that thinks it is real. Maybe free will is just a biological bias, meant to help us remain active, while it is not more than an illusion [Free will is an evolutionary illusion](/notes/free-will-is-an-evolutionary-illusion.md).
