# Mind-Body Duality

## Notes


### Claim

There is a separation between your body and your soul/mind.

### Explanation

According to this view, it is logical to assume that experiences in your body are external to "who you are", they are not indicative of your identity. For example - emotions, urges and hormones are considered as external forces trying to influence your consciousness [Achieving internal harmony requires integrating all parts of yourself](/notes/achieving-internal-harmony-requires-integrating-all-parts-of-yourself.md).

If this is true, than morality is only the result of logical inference [Truth is discoverable through reason and logic alone](/notes/truth-is-discoverable-through-reason-and-logic-alone.md), and doesn't involve any other type of "signals" such as [Some knowledge requires direct experience to fully understand](/notes/some-knowledge-requires-direct-experience-to-fully-understand.md). Meaning that it goes against the idea that [Morality is both rational and emotional](/notes/morality-is-both-rational-and-emotional.md)

### Supporters

[The hard problem of consciousness](/notes/subjective-experience-resists-scientific-explanation.md) is probably the strongest case for this view — no physical description of neurons seems to explain why there's something it feels like to be conscious at all.

The feeling that ["I" am not my body, just its controller](/notes/multidimensional-identity-collapses-into-single-fixed-characteristic.md) shows up naturally when we feel alienated from our own body, as if there's a true self sitting behind it.

### Opposers

[The mind-body connection](/notes/physical-states-and-actions-unconsciously-shape-beliefs-and-behavior.md) argues the opposite — that the body doesn't just carry the mind around, but actively shapes what it believes and how it behaves.

[Naturalism](/notes/everything-in-the-world-is-a-natural-phenomenon.md) removes the soul from the picture entirely: if nothing is supernatural, there's no separate, non-physical substance left for the mind to be distinct from.
