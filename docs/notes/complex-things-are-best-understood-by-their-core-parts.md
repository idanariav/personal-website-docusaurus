# Reductionism

## Notes

### Claim
According to reductionism, many of life's properties, can be understood, explained, or better viewed as when we [distill](/notes/distilling-separates-essence-from-noise-to-simplify-understanding.md) it to it's core.

### Explanation
For example, questions about meaning and happiness seems too big to tackle, but perhaps a good enough explanation for our reasons for action could be explained through the lens of [Utility quantifies measurable benefits and guides rational decisions](/notes/utility-quantifies-measurable-benefits-and-guides-rational-decisions.md), to reduce the question of happiness to a simple pain/pleasure framework.

### Why it matters
This is also why [Breaking complex topics into smaller units improves learning and mastery](/notes/breaking-complex-topics-into-smaller-units-improves-learning-and-mastery.md) — once you know a thing's core parts, you can learn and master it piece by piece instead of facing the whole undifferentiated mass at once.

### Examples
A clear case of this in ethics is [utilitarianism](/notes/maximizing-pleasure-and-minimizing-suffering-defines-moral-action.md), which reduces the sprawling question of what makes an action moral down to a single measurable quantity: net pleasure minus pain.

### Supporters
Rather than believe that [truth](/notes/truth.md) is an [emergent](/notes/system-properties-arise-from-component-interactions-not-individual-parts.md) property, it means to believe that it is in the details. That to [simplify](/notes/distilling-to-essentials-creates-clarity-and-actionability.md) problems into small manageable units is not only better it's also more correct [Simplify the problem before trying to solve it](/notes/simplify-the-problem-before-trying-to-solve-it.md). This is essentially the atomist stance: [Understanding requires breaking things to their smallest parts](/notes/understanding-requires-breaking-things-to-their-smallest-parts.md) argues a system's features are just its smallest components, nothing hidden beyond them.

### Opposers
[The whole is greater than its parts](/notes/the-whole-is-greater-than-its-parts.md) — some properties are emergent, arising from the interactions between components rather than the components themselves, so breaking something down can lose exactly what makes it what it is.
